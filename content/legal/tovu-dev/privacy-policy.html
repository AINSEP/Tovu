3. What this site collects

Only forms and the site chat are in use today. Everything else below is a capability of the software that is switched off here; we describe it because we may switch it on, and we say so where we do.

3.1 Forms — active

When you submit a form we store the values you typed, the time, and your IP address in full, unhashed. That is the one place on this site a raw IP is written down; we use it to rate-limit and to identify abuse. IP addresses on form submissions are deleted after 90 days. The answers and timestamp remain until the submission is deleted. To request deletion of a submission, use the contact form.

A failed submission sets a tovu_form_flash cookie for 120 seconds so your answers survive the reload. It holds only what you just typed, is HttpOnly, and is same-origin. Forms also carry a hidden spam-trap field; if it comes back filled the submission is discarded.

3.2 Site chat — active, and it sends what you type to an AI provider

The chat widget on every page sends what you type to the AI model provider we have configured, to generate a reply. That provider handles it under its own terms and privacy policy. Today that provider is Google (Gemini API). The software can also use Anthropic, OpenAI, Azure OpenAI, OpenRouter, or any compatible endpoint; if we switch, we will update this section and the table in section 4.

Do not type anything into the chat you would not send to a third party. Its answers are generated by AI and can be wrong.

The key is ours, not yours — you supply nothing and see nothing. Requests are rate-limited using your IP address, which is read for that check and not stored. Your conversation is kept in your browser's sessionStorage: it is per-tab, it dies when you close the tab, and it never reaches us as a stored record.

3.3 Analytics — capability present, no events recorded

No page on this site loads an analytics script today, so nothing is recorded. If we enable analytics it is our own, first-party, and sets no cookie. It is not Google Analytics and not an advertising pixel.

It does not store your IP address or User-Agent. Before anything is written, your IP is truncated to a coarse network block, combined with a broad device class and a salt that rotates daily, and hashed one way. The structure that reaches storage has no field for an IP or a User-Agent, so neither can leak downstream by mistake. Because the salt rotates daily you cannot be followed from one day to the next; there is no persistent visitor ID and no cross-site identifier. Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track are honored. Property names and values that look like personal data are rejected before storage.

Stored: a page path, a coarse device class, browser and OS family, a referring hostname, campaign tags, an event name, and the daily hash. Country and region columns exist but are always empty — we do no geolocation.

3.4 Newsletter — capability present, no subscriptions

There is no signup box on this site, and no public route that creates a subscription. If we ever add you to a list, your subscription starts pending and becomes active only when you click a confirmation link. The subscription record holds a reference to your identity record, not your email. Confirmation tokens are stored only as hashes, with an expiry. Unsubscribing is a public, one-click, signed link that needs no login and sets no cookie — it changes the status, it does not erase the record.

3.5 Comments — capability present, not in use

No comment has ever been stored here. If we turn them on: comments are anonymous, we store the name you give plus optional email and website, and your IP is never stored — it is hashed with a secret salt as the request arrives. Spam filtering is entirely local; nothing is sent to a third-party scoring service. Comments can be permanently purged, and that purge is a real hard delete.

3.6 Member accounts — capability present, no accounts

Sign-in would be passwordless, by emailed one-time link. There is no password anywhere in this system. We would store your email, optionally a name, verification state, status, and an internal note. Sessions store a hash of the session token — never the token — plus, unlike the rest of the site, your IP and User-Agent in full, for session security. Consent would be recorded as a purpose-by-purpose ledger, with withdrawal recorded as its own entry rather than by erasing the original.

Member records cannot be deleted. They are designed to be disabled, never destroyed, because a member is an actor in the site's revision history. There is no code path anywhere that removes one. Section 10 explains what we do instead.

3.7 Media

Images here are uploaded by us; there is no public upload. Publicly served images are re-encoded, which does not carry EXIF or GPS metadata into what you download. Original files are stored as received, metadata intact. If you send us an image, assume it still carries whatever your camera put in it.

3.8 Payments — not operational

Nothing can be bought here. The only payment provider wired in is a non-functional reference implementation with no gateway behind it. There are zero order records. Card details could never reach our database in any case — the order record has no field for a card number, billing address, or cardholder name, only opaque processor references. One caveat if we ever enable it: processor notifications are stored verbatim as received, and some processors put a customer name or email in them.

3.9 Logs

We log errors, not request bodies, IPs, or email addresses. We have not audited every error path, so it is conceivable an error message could carry a fragment of something you typed. We do not use logs to build profiles and we do not share them.

10. Erasure, exactly

Permanently and irreversibly destroyed on request: your form submissions, including the IP stored with them; your comments, via the purge function.

Only disabled and overwritten: member accounts. The software cannot delete a member record — a member participates in the site's revision history, and destroying the record would corrupt it, so the capability was deliberately never built. What we do instead is disable the account and overwrite the identifying fields, so what remains no longer identifies you. We will confirm when it is done.

Kept: consent records, where we need them to show consent was properly obtained and withdrawn — Art. 17(3) permits this. Records the law requires us to keep. Analytics events, which carry no identifier that could be traced back to you.

Erasure applies to live systems immediately and to any backups as they age out of rotation. We do not restore erased data into a live system.

13. Security

What is structurally true, rather than reassuring adjectives:

  • No passwords exist in this system. There is no password database to breach.
  • Session tokens are never stored — only a hash, so nothing in the database can be replayed as a login.
  • Newsletter confirmation tokens are stored as hashes, with an expiry.
  • Analytics discards personal data before storage by construction: the structure that reaches storage has no IP or User-Agent field.
  • Comment IPs are hashed at the network boundary, before storage.
  • All cookies are HttpOnly, so no JavaScript on the page can read them.
  • Member sessions and administrator sessions are architecturally isolated and cannot read one another. Deleting a visitor's data is a permission that must be explicitly granted.
  • Traffic is served over HTTPS.

What we do not claim: no security certification, no third-party penetration test, no guarantee. If you find a vulnerability, please report it using the contact form before disclosing it publicly.

If a personal data breach is likely to risk your rights and freedoms, we will notify the supervisory authority as the law requires: under GDPR Art. 33, without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware. If it is likely to be high risk to you, we will contact you without undue delay, as Art. 34 requires, and tell you what happened, what was involved, and what you can do. We do not promise a shorter deadline than the law sets.

14. Self-hosted Tovu — you are the controller

If you run Tovu yourself, everything above is essentially irrelevant to your install.

Your install sends us nothing. No content, no visitor data, no configuration, no usage, no crash reports. Your database is yours.

It does talk to third parties. These go straight from your machine to them, not through us:

  • GitHub — the desktop app checks GitHub for a new release shortly after launch and then every few hours, and downloads updates from there. GitHub sees your IP address, the app version, and your platform. Set TOVU_DESKTOP_DISABLE_UPDATER=1 to turn this off.
  • The npm registry — when you install Tovu, a plugin, or another package.
  • The AI provider you pick — see below.
  • Any service you connect — for example a publishing host, a source-control account, or an external tool server. Each gets what you send it, under its own terms.

You are the data controller for your site. Every mechanism in section 3 runs in your install and writes to your database. You decide what to enable. You need your own privacy policy — do not copy this one, it describes our choices. We are not your processor, and there is nothing for us to sign a data processing agreement about. (A hosted Tovu would change that; see section 17.)

The admin AI assistant uses the provider you pick. Your content, prompts, and any files or pages the assistant reads go to that provider, under your account and its terms. It does not route through us. The software supports:

  • Your own API key with Anthropic, OpenAI, Azure OpenAI, Google Gemini, OpenRouter, or any compatible endpoint you enter.
  • A local model through Ollama, which stays on your machine.
  • A coding-agent CLI already installed on your machine, such as Claude Code or Codex. That tool sends content to its own vendor under its own terms.

AI you connect is not ours. The AI providers, coding-agent CLIs, and tool (MCP) servers you connect process your data under their own terms and privacy policies, not this one. To the fullest extent permitted by law, we are not responsible for how they handle, keep, or use it, or for leaks or exposure through them or through what an AI agent does with the access you give it. You choose them and what they can reach. Terms of Service section 11.1 has more.

Things worth checking in your own install:

  • The visitor site chat is off by default and costs money when on. If you enable it, whatever your visitors type goes to the model provider you configured, on your key. Disclose that in your own policy.
  • Set a real secret for COMMENTS_IP_SALT. Left unset with comments enabled, the software falls back to a publicly documented development value and your commenters' IP hashes become trivially reversible.
  • Check what your theme actually loads. A theme manifest that declares web fonts can, on some render paths, make every page fetch them from Google and send your visitors' IPs there. Read your own rendered HTML rather than assuming. (The theme this site runs does not.)
  • Decide whether to honor Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control in the analytics configuration.
  • Member records cannot be deleted in your install either. If you take on members, plan for anonymization rather than deletion.
