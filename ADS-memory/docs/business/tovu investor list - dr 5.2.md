# AI-Native CMS / Web Infrastructure Investor Target List and Contact Map

Research date: February 20, 2026 (America/Los_Angeles)

## Research scope and scoring framework

This research prioritizes **people** (decision-makers + high-leverage operator-investors) who are plausibly aligned with an **AI-native CMS / web infrastructure platform** thesis: AI-first architecture, developer-first distribution loops, open-source GTM, strong security boundaries (sandboxing/permissions), agent-ready UX/workflows, and ecosystem/network effects via plugins and monetization surfaces.

Targets were selected from a universe of early-stage investors, developer-infrastructure specialists, AI-native investors, and a small set of “builder/studio/platform” investors who can materially help distribution and hiring—not just capital—based on their stated focus, portfolio adjacency, and public engagement. citeturn6view0turn21search9turn14search3turn19search6turn25search33turn37search0

### Evidence standards used
Claims about focus, stage, portfolio, and CMS exposure are grounded in **primary sources** whenever available (firm bios, firm pages, investment announcements, portfolio pages, official blog posts, and show notes). citeturn26search2turn26search3turn40view0turn39search0turn39search26

### Scoring model and ranking method
Each person was scored on a 0–100 composite and ordered accordingly (the table is already sorted by priority). The default weights requested were used:

- Thesis fit (AI-native/platform/agents/devtools): 22%
- Stage fit (pre-seed/seed/A): 18%
- Lead likelihood (ability + history of leading rounds): 15%
- Domain adjacency (CMS/web infra/commerce/open-source): 12%
- CMS category familiarity: 10%
- Conflict risk (high score = low conflict): 8%
- Activity recency: 10%
- Geography fit: 3%
- Outreach accessibility: 2%

Numeric columns in the table are presented as **subscores (0–10)** for the most actionable dimensions, and the final ordering reflects the full weighted score.

## Executive summary

- **AI-native specialist funds** (explicitly “AI-native / Software 3.0”) are structurally best aligned for an “AI-first CMS/web platform” pitch because they already underwrite product-native AI workflows and “agentic” UI shifts, rather than retrofits. citeturn6view0  
- The highest-conviction **developer-infrastructure funds** tend to respond best to “platform + ecosystem” framing: open-source adoption loops, extensibility, and a path to network effects via plugins/markets. citeturn14search3turn19search6turn21search9  
- Investors with **verified headless CMS / web-platform exposure** cluster around a few key bets: Contentful, Strapi, Sanity, and Webflow—use these as shorthand for category understanding, but watch for conflicts. citeturn39search0turn40view0turn39search26turn39search3  
- **CMS exposure is mostly “positive adjacency”** (they understand content distribution and developer workflows). The main **conflict risk** is when a fund is deeply invested in a direct headless CMS competitor (e.g., Strapi, Sanity, Contentful). citeturn40view0turn39search26turn39search0  
- Firms that publicly position themselves as “first investor” / “inception” / “from day one” are disproportionately compatible with a thesis that includes **migration wedges** and **ecosystem build-out**, because they underwrite long arcs. citeturn19search6turn21search9turn37search0  
- Contact availability is uneven: a minority publish direct emails, but several European funds list partner emails (notably Balderton), and some investors explicitly publish personal emails on blogs. citeturn26search2turn32view0turn26search3turn26search5  
- Your most repeatable “why now” angle across investor archetypes: AI is changing the *unit of software*, so the **CMS becomes an AI execution/runtime layer** (permissions, tools, adapters) rather than a page database. This mirrors how some headless CMS vendors already argue that “CMS” is evolving into a broader content/data system. citeturn39search10  
- For devtools/infra investors, “**secure plugin sandboxing** + permissions” is a compelling wedge because it turns AI agents/plugins into governable infrastructure, not a UX gimmick. Tie to supply-chain security and platform control narratives. citeturn14search3turn21search9  
- For CMS-adjacent investors, avoid implying “we’re just a better headless CMS.” Instead: “**AI-native web app platform** with migration wedge + marketplace economics.” (Many already have headless CMS exposure; they will compare you immediately.) citeturn40view0turn39search0  
- Common outreach channels that are reliably available: (1) direct emails on partner pages/blogs, (2) firm intake forms (20VC, Seedcamp), (3) firm inboxes for inquiries (Crane, Amplify, Index press). citeturn26search2turn32view0turn28view1turn42search16turn37search3turn41search2turn20search13  
- What to avoid in first touch: “we’re replacing WordPress” as the headline. Replace it with “migration wedge from legacy CMS into an agent-ready, secure plugin runtime,” then quantify developer adoption loops. (Most investors dislike undifferentiated incumbent-bashing without a wedge + proof.) citeturn40view0turn21search9turn19search6  
- Best warm-intro strategy: route through **portfolio founders** and **devtool communities** (events, OSS maintainers) because many of these funds explicitly emphasize community and founder support. citeturn14search3turn37search12turn19search6

## Outreach messaging angles

These templates are designed to be sent as **first-touch cold emails** (or lightly adapted for LinkedIn/X), each with a tight hook + why now + why this shape wins + CTA.

### AI-native “Software 3.0” investor angle
Hook: We’re not adding AI to a CMS—we’re rebuilding the CMS/web stack so AI agents are first-class.  
Why now: As models get good enough to plan/act, the missing piece is a **governed runtime** where agents can safely use tools, write content, and trigger commerce without turning the website into an un-auditable mess.  
Why we win: We’re TypeScript-first and open-source, with a permissions/sandbox model and hexagonal adapters so enterprises can swap infra without rewriting everything. Our migration wedge makes adoption non-theoretical.  
CTA: If you’re open to it, I’d love to share a short demo and get your pushback on whether “agent-runtime CMS” becomes a new platform category.

### Devtools investor angle
Hook: We’re building the developer-first infrastructure that turns “AI + websites” from prototypes into production systems.  
Why now: Developer workflows are being rewritten by agents, but the web stack is still built around legacy CMS assumptions (monoliths, weak isolation, brittle plugins).  
Why we win: Our architecture treats plugins/agents as untrusted code with permissions, sandboxing, and adapters—so developers can safely extend and monetize the platform.  
CTA: Can I send a 2-minute Loom that shows the plugin sandbox + migration flow?

### Open-source GTM + ecosystem angle
Hook: We’re building an OSS core where adoption compounds into a plugin ecosystem—then into monetization.  
Why now: The winners in modern infrastructure often start with OSS distribution, but AI changes “what gets extended”: agents, workflows, and monetizable interfaces.  
Why we win: We’ve designed for extensibility as the moat (plugin permissions, adapter layer) and for monetization as a first-class surface (conversational commerce/ads/plugins).  
CTA: If you’re open, I’d love feedback on how you’d sequence ecosystem vs monetization milestones.

### Security-first “plugin sandboxing” angle
Hook: The next big security problem is “untrusted agent + untrusted plugin” inside core business workflows. We’re making that governable.  
Why now: As teams deploy internal agents and website agents, the permission boundary becomes the product.  
Why we win: Sandbox + permissions at the core, not bolted on; strong isolation that enables enterprises to say “yes” to extensibility.  
CTA: Would you be open to a 20-minute technical walkthrough with our architecture diagram + threat model?

### “Incumbent replacement” wedge angle
Hook: This is a migration-led replacement of CMS architecture, not a greenfield tool hoping for adoption.  
Why now: AI makes legacy CMS UX and plugin models feel increasingly obsolete; teams want content + commerce + support workflows unified with agents.  
Why we win: We start where budgets already exist—migrations—and convert legacy sites into an agent-ready, extensible runtime with a clear upgrade path.  
CTA: If I share the migration story and wedge metrics, can you tell me what would make this “inevitable” vs “nice to have”?
