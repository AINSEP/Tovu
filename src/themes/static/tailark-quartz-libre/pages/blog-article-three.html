Sustainability in tech: a growing focus

Explore efforts towards sustainable practices in technology.

Alden Cho · October 29, 2025

Software has a footprint. It's easy to forget that when the interface is a browser tab, but every request, render, and background job draws power from a data center somewhere — and that somewhere increasingly cares about how much it's using.

The footprint behind the cloud

Data centers now account for a meaningful and growing share of global electricity use, driven largely by demand for compute-heavy workloads. Cloud providers have responded with real investment in renewable-powered regions and more efficient cooling — but the underlying growth curve means efficiency gains are running to stand still.

Efficiency as a design constraint

The teams making the most progress treat energy use like any other budget: something to measure and optimize, not an afterthought. That shows up in small, concrete choices — caching aggressively, batching background jobs instead of polling, choosing regions with cleaner grids, and retiring idle infrastructure instead of letting it linger.

None of this requires a sustainability team. It requires the same instincts that already make software faster and cheaper to run — which is probably why it's spreading.

Why reporting is catching up

Regulation is starting to require what good practice already recommended: real disclosure of energy use and emissions tied to software operations, not just corporate offices and travel. That's pushing sustainability out of the marketing page and into the same dashboards engineering teams already watch for cost and latency.

The honest version of this story isn't dramatic — it's a lot of unglamorous efficiency work, compounding.
